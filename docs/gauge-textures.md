# Gauge textures

Textures for the planned GUI fill gauge, styled as a glass graduated cylinder. They live in
`assets/mmdlib/textures/gui/gauge/` and are generated by `scripts/generate_gauge_textures.py`
(standard library only; run `python3 scripts/generate_gauge_textures.py [thickness]`, default 18).
`gui.client.GaugeRenderer` draws them; this page describes its texture contract.

These textures supply `<gauge>` and the matching slim background of `<energy-gauge>`. The energy
renderer draws thin red charge bars with a soft reflection, without a terminal or graduations.
Bars are at most three pixels thick, separated by one-pixel gaps.
Both empty interiors use `#808080`, matching the flat centre of the recessed slot sprite.

## Files

All textures are drawn for a **vertical** gauge 18 px thick. Horizontal gauges use the same
files rotated 90° clockwise (see below).

| File | Size | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `body_top.png` | 18×5 | Top cap of the tube: thin outline and light grey interior, rounded corners |
| `body_middle.png` | 18×1 | One row of the tube, repeated to fill the length |
| `body_bottom.png` | 18×5 | Bottom cap |
| `glass_top.png`, `glass_middle.png`, `glass_bottom.png` | as above | Cylindrical lighting: dark curved sides, a broad lit face, and an off-centre vertical reflection |
| `digits.png` | 39×5 | White 3×5 numeral atlas; not used by the gauge renderer |

Pixels outside the rounded shape are fully transparent, so the GUI background shows through
the corners.

## Draw order

For a gauge of length *L* (≥ 10) at (*x*, *y*):

1. **Body**: `body_top` at the top, `body_middle` repeated *L* − 10 times, `body_bottom` at the end.
2. **Contents**, filling from the bottom: fluid uses its still sprite
   (`IClientFluidTypeExtensions.getStillTexture`) multiplied by its tint (`getTintColor`), tiled
   in 16 px steps from the bottom edge; energy is a solid colour. Lighten the top row of the
   contents by 30% toward white as a meniscus (vertical gauges only).
3. **Glass**: flush preceding buffered GUI draws, enable source-alpha blending, then draw
   the three `glass_*` pieces in the same arrangement as the body. The renderer restores the
   caller's blend enable/function, depth-test state and shader color after drawing the gauge.
   The overlay is clipped to the filled portion, leaving the empty interior neutral slot grey.
   Fluid textures remain visible beneath it; the cylinder effect comes from lighting, not a new
   outline. The energy gauge uses only the body textures, without this glass overlay.
4. **Graduations**, drawn in code: colour `#EEF3F6` at 85% opacity, with a 35% black shadow
   pixel directly below each mark pixel. From the left inside edge (x = 1):
   - every 5%: 2 px; every 10%: 3 px; 25/50/75: 7 px (40% of the 16 px inside width)
   - drop the 5% marks when they would be under 3 px apart, then the 10% marks

The scale shows tick marks only, without numeric labels. Exact amounts remain in the tooltip.

A mark at fraction *p* sits on row `1 + inner - round(inner * p)`, where `inner = L - 2`.

## Clipping contents to the rounded inside

Inside the caps the interior narrows. Clip contents per row to these interior columns
(top cap shown; the bottom cap mirrors it, so its last row is row 0):

| Cap row | Interior columns |
|---|---|
| 0 | none (outline) |
| 1 | 2–15 |
| 2–4 | 1–16 |

Every `body_middle` row's interior is columns 1–16.

## Horizontal gauges

Draw everything rotated 90° clockwise: the bottom cap becomes the left cap, the top cap the
right cap, the highlight lands on top, and contents fill left to right. Marks hang down from
the top edge. The fluid sprite stays upright (tile it unrotated). Skip the meniscus.

An interactive mockup of the design was used to agree it; these files reproduce its pixels.

## Compact segmented gauge

A slim, LED-style alternative to the glass gauge: fixed thickness, any length, a row of
tinted segments separated by thin gaps. Two thicknesses are supported: **5 px** and **10 px**.
It is almost entirely drawn in code; the only texture is the frame sprite `mmdlib:compact_frame`
(8×8, nine-slice border 1, transparent middle; see [GUI sprites](gui-sprites.md)).

Defaults: segments **2 px** long, gaps **1 px**.

### Draw order

For thickness *T* and length *L* (vertical; horizontal swaps the axes):

1. Fill the whole rectangle with the **gap colour**: the panel's background colour, or black
   when none is specified.
2. Draw `compact_frame` stretched to *T*×*L*: dark top and left edges, light bottom and right.
3. Draw the segments as plain filled rectangles inside the frame.

### Segment layout

Along the length, the usable space is `L - 2` (inside the frame). It starts with one gap, and
every segment is followed by a gap:

```
n     = max(1, round((usable - gap) / (segment + gap)))
space = usable - gap - n * gap
size(i) = floor(space / n) + (i < space % n ? 1 : 0)
```

So the segment count is the one closest to the target size, segments stay within a pixel of it,
and they fill the length exactly. Segment 0 sits at the bottom (left when horizontal), starting
`1 + gap` pixels in from the end.

Across the gauge, segments span the whole inside of the frame (`1` to `T - 2`) for thicknesses
under 12 px. Thicker gauges keep a 1 px gap-coloured margin on each side.

### Fill and colour

- Lit segments: `round(fill * n)`, clamped so the gauge reads **empty only when empty and full
  only when full**: any amount above zero lights at least one segment, and anything below full
  leaves at least one unlit.
- Lit segments use the tint colour; unlit ones the tint at 28% brightness.
- Segments are flat. Only segments at least 4 px in both directions get a bevel (top/left edge
  35% toward white, bottom/right edge at 70%; 12% and 85% when unlit). Smaller segments drawn
  with a bevel blur into the gaps and their proportions read wrong.
